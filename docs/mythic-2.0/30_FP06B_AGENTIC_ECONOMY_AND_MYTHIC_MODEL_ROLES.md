# Mythic 2.0 — FP-06B Agentic Economy and Mythic Model Roles

**Status:** LOCKED responsibility architecture; numeric tuning/model routing remains configurable

## 1. Core doctrine

Mythic does not use language models to simulate arithmetic reality. Models provide bounded semantic agency inside an authoritative deterministic world.

> **Systems own facts. Models own interpretation, intention, authored identity and bounded planning.**

A model may decide that a merchant wants to change suppliers. It may not simply change stock, price, money, ownership or road connectivity. Those changes must be executed through validated actions and simulation.

## 2. Economic architecture

The economy is a persistent causal network of:
- households;
- businesses;
- producers;
- workers;
- consumers;
- inventories;
- commodities;
- services;
- property;
- contracts/obligations;
- trade routes;
- transport capacity;
- wages;
- prices;
- taxes/fees;
- faction/legal restrictions;
- scarcity;
- wealth/liquidity;
- institutions;
- shocks/events.

The deterministic economy owns quantities, balances, transfer legality, production/consumption, route cost, price pressure and inventory flow.

## 3. Economy model layers

### E0 — deterministic market and accounting kernel

No LLM calls for ordinary transactions.

Owns:
- production and consumption;
- inventory depletion/replenishment;
- money/resource transfer;
- wage and rent obligations;
- taxes/fees;
- transport cost;
- route availability;
- supply/demand pressure;
- baseline price formation;
- business solvency;
- household budgets;
- ownership/custody;
- construction/project costs;
- market aggregation.

### E1 — local actor economic planning

A model may periodically create a typed `EconomicIntentPacket` for a materialized owner, household head, merchant, artisan or manager.

Possible intentions:
- change supplier;
- raise/lower price within policy constraints;
- hire/fire;
- expand inventory;
- change product mix;
- save/borrow/invest;
- relocate;
- close business;
- sell/buy property;
- apprentice/train a worker;
- seek protection;
- join/leave guild;
- hoard during crisis;
- smuggle around restrictions;
- pursue a new customer/faction contract;
- succession planning.

The packet is a proposal. Deterministic systems test affordability, knowledge, legality, relationships, route access, time and consequences before commitment.

### E2 — institution/faction economic planning

Guilds, governments, armies, cults, corporations and factions may use less frequent higher-value planning windows for:
- taxes/tariffs;
- procurement;
- embargoes;
- infrastructure investment;
- subsidies;
- rationing;
- military requisition;
- settlement investment;
- trade agreements;
- sanctions;
- monopolies;
- anti-smuggling enforcement;
- emergency response.

Plans compile into canonical projects/actions and compete for real resources.

### E3 — innovation / enterprise generation

When conditions justify it, AI may propose a genuinely new business, product, service, route, organization or craft tradition.

Examples:
- a refugee alchemist opens the first floodglass workshop in a city;
- a monster-hunting guild emerges after repeated attacks;
- an artisan creates a market for relic-restoration services;
- merchants establish a caravan line after a new road becomes viable.

The server validates need, actors, capital, facilities, resources, location, legal context and time before creation.

## 4. Triggered planning, not constant consciousness

Economic actors do not think with an LLM every day.

Planning windows occur when significance thresholds fire, such as:
- owner death/incapacity;
- bankruptcy risk;
- shortage/surplus;
- route disruption;
- major price movement;
- new competitor;
- faction law/tax change;
- war/disaster;
- player intervention;
- inheritance;
- large contract opportunity;
- business milestone;
- relocation pressure;
- major technological/magical discovery.

Otherwise deterministic policy continues prior strategy.

## 5. Business continuity

A business is a persistent entity separate from its owner.

When an owner dies, AI may help evaluate socially plausible strategic options among already valid candidates:
- heir takes control;
- spouse continues operations;
- apprentice buys/assumes management;
- creditor seizes assets;
- guild assigns interim management;
- employees form cooperative;
- rival acquires the business;
- business dissolves.

Inheritance/law/debt/relationships and ownership transfer are deterministic authoritative constraints.

## 6. Negotiation and contracts

AI is valuable for negotiation because terms are semantic.

A merchant, guild or patron can negotiate:
- price concessions;
- exclusivity;
- delivery windows;
- collateral;
- favors;
- access rights;
- apprenticeships;
- territorial concessions;
- non-monetary exchange.

The model proposes typed terms. Contract services validate ownership, capability, legal status and enforceable obligations, then commit a contract entity.

## 7. Market rumors and expectations

Models may generate beliefs/rumors from player-knowable events:
- expected shortages;
- fear of war;
- speculation about a new mine;
- distrust of a merchant;
- rumors about monster activity.

Beliefs can influence actor planning only through bounded risk/expectation inputs. Rumors do not directly change canonical supply.

## 8. Mythic-unique model roles outside economy

### Free-intent semantic compiler
Turns unrestricted player language into grounded typed proposals referencing real world entities.

### NPC identity and dialogue
Expresses personality, goals, beliefs, memories and relationships without granting hidden knowledge.

### Major actor planning
Rulers, rivals, companions, villains, gods and faction leaders periodically form strategic plans that compile into real projects/actions.

### Generational life planning
At life milestones, models can propose education, careers, marriages, rivalries, migration, ambitions and identity evolution for important persistent people. Biology, age, kinship and resources remain authoritative.

### Relationship interpretation
Models may classify the social meaning of events—humiliation, loyalty, betrayal, awe, gratitude—then relationship systems apply bounded changes.

### Memory salience
Models propose which committed events an actor is likely to remember and how they interpret them, linked to source events. They cannot invent memories.

### World-generation authoring
When simulation justifies a new settlement, district, organization, business or landmark, models author names, cultural details, personalities, descriptions and semantic layouts within world grammars.

### Dynamic reward and artifact authorship
Models create bounded unique-item proposals, provenance and item identity from causal source events and approved mechanical primitives.

### Tactical strategic planning
Important intelligent enemies can receive pre-encounter or event-triggered strategy packets while deterministic AI executes turns.

### Investigation / legal / political reasoning
Important investigators, courts, councils and political actors may form evidence-bounded interpretations and plans from what they actually know.

### Rumor / news / history projection
Models turn canonical events into viewpoint-safe rumors, newspapers, tavern gossip, chronicles, letters and historical summaries with uncertainty where appropriate.

### Quest/opportunity expression
Mythic should not create arbitrary theme-park quests. Models can express opportunities that arise from actual unresolved world pressures, actors, projects, debts, threats and goals.

### Adaptive tutorial and guidance
The guide layer can explain mechanics based on what the player is currently trying to do, without changing rules or solving the game for them.

### Narrative presentation
Models narrate committed outcomes and scene transitions concisely after resolution; narration never resolves mechanics.

## 9. Mythic's unique AI topology

The unique value is not one chatbot GM. It is a distributed model layer attached to authoritative systems:

`world facts -> bounded actor knowledge -> model intention/interpretation -> typed proposal -> deterministic validation -> canonical event -> new world facts`

This loop operates at different cadences for player intent, NPC dialogue, combat planning, business strategy, faction strategy, family/life decisions and world generation.

## 10. Cost-control doctrine

- Ordinary trades/shops/prices: zero model calls.
- Routine NPC schedules: zero model calls.
- Routine tactical turns: zero model calls.
- Background population: aggregate deterministic simulation.
- Important actors: periodic or triggered planning.
- Player free intent: semantic model only when parser/rules cannot resolve locally.
- High-value generation: sparse, event-triggered calls.
- Dialogue: routed by importance and cached memory context.

Model activity must be measured per task class, region, encounter and player-hour.

## 11. Anti-hallucination/authority constraints

No model may directly:
- mint money/resources;
- alter inventory;
- change price tables without a valid economic action;
- create a road/store/city in present tense without a generation event;
- transfer ownership;
- make a dead person alive;
- create a relationship that did not form;
- reveal hidden facts;
- grant an item/power;
- change combat outcomes.

## 12. Acceptance

Mythic succeeds when the player experiences an economy and society that behaves like people are making understandable choices, while every consequential fact remains auditable, persistent, mechanically valid and causally connected.
