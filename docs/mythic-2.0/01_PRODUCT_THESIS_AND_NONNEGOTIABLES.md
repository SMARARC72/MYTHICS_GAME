# Mythic 2.0 — Product Thesis and Non-Negotiables

## Player promise

> **Attempt anything plausible. The world decides what happens. The world remembers.**

The player is not limited to authored dialogue trees or predefined action buttons. The player is also not a co-author who may declare convenient facts into existence.

## Five product pillars

### 1. Grounded freedom

Natural language allows players to express methods the UI did not anticipate. The action must ground to current character capability, visible/known entities, geography, inventory, relationships or other authoritative world state.

### 2. Mechanical authority

Dice, modifiers, stats, inventory, damage, conditions, progression, action economy, spatial position, time, ownership and other mechanical truth are resolved by deterministic systems—not invented by narration.

### 3. Persistent causal world

Actions propagate reasonable downstream effects. NPCs die, age, marry, relocate, inherit, change jobs, gain power, lose property, remember people and alter institutions. Businesses, roads, factions, settlements and economies may change.

### 4. A real RPG before an AI novelty

The game must remain fun if the player stops being impressed by generative AI. Combat, progression, loot, builds, powers, exploration, resource tradeoffs, relationships, discovery and risk must stand on their own.

### 5. World continuity beyond the hero

The world survives death and retirement. Generational play is architected from the beginning even if full multigenerational breadth is not required for the first Early Access release.

## Non-negotiable invariants

1. The AI cannot silently retcon committed canonical state.
2. No interactable object exists only because a narrator mentioned it.
3. A generated future fact must have provenance: action, actor, rule, world event or approved generation process.
4. Hidden knowledge remains hidden from actors who should not know it.
5. Death and failure are real outcomes.
6. Existing objects, people, businesses and places retain stable identities through change.
7. Visual state is a projection of canonical state, not the canonical state itself.
8. Normal play uses player-facing RPG language, not engineering vocabulary.
9. Free intent is always available even when convenient buttons/actions exist.
10. Every major mechanic must justify its fun, simulation value or product differentiation.

## Player-facing tone

Narration defaults to concise, vivid and understandable language. Complexity belongs in simulation, not prose.

## Adoption strategy

Use familiar d20/5E concepts where they remove unnecessary learning burden:

- d20 + modifier vs DC;
- Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma where compatible;
- HP;
- Armor Class/Defense;
- initiative;
- ability checks;
- saving throws;
- advantage/disadvantage;
- proficiency/training;
- damage dice;
- conditions;
- critical results.

Keep Mythic-specific systems only where they create meaningful differentiation.
